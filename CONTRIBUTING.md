# Adding a use case

This list collects real examples of charities and other non-profits using AI, with what worked and what to watch out for. If you know of one, please add it. You do not need to be technical, and you do not need to have built the project yourself.

There are two ways to contribute. Pick whichever is easier for you.

## The easy way: tell us about it

1. Open a [new proposal](https://github.com/KindTechUK/awesome-charity-ai-use-cases/issues/new/choose) and choose "Propose something new".
2. Under "What kind of change is it?", pick "A use case for the list".
3. Fill in what you know, using the questions under [What we need to know](#what-we-need-to-know). Gaps are fine.

A maintainer will write the entry, add it to the list and credit you. You need a free GitHub account to open a proposal.

## The direct way: add it yourself

You can do this in your browser. Nothing needs installing.

1. Open [README.md](README.md) and select the pencil icon to edit it.
2. Find the section that fits, such as "Data analysis" or "Chatbots". If none fits, add your entry at the end of the closest one and say so in your description. We will sort out the section.
3. Copy the template below, paste it at the end of that section, and fill it in.
4. Select "Commit changes", then "Propose changes", then "Create pull request". Say in a sentence or two why this example is worth sharing.

Please add one use case per pull request. It makes each one quicker to review.

### Template

```markdown
* [Name of the charity and what they did](https://link-to-a-public-write-up)
  * **Challenge**: The problem they had, in one sentence
  * **Solution**: What they built or used, and which AI tools
  * **Key Learnings**:
    - What worked
    - What did not, or what they would do differently
    - **Risks**: What could go wrong, and how they guard against it
  * **Impact**: What changed as a result, with a link if there is evidence
```

Leave out any line you cannot fill in honestly. A short, true entry is better than a long one with guesses.

## What we need to know

- **Who did it.** A charity, community group, social enterprise or public body, or a team working for one.
- **A public link.** A blog post, report, talk, code repository or news article that anyone can open. We cannot list something that only exists in a private document.
- **The problem.** What was taking too long, costing too much or not possible before.
- **What they did.** Which tools they used and how people stayed involved, for example a supervisor checking answers before they reach a client.
- **What they learned.** Including what went wrong. The honest parts are the most useful to other charities.
- **The risks.** Privacy, bias, wrong answers, cost, or dependence on one supplier, and what was done about each.

## What belongs on the list

- Real projects that have been used, piloted or openly tested. Ideas and product announcements do not count yet.
- Examples from any country, as long as the write-up is public.
- Projects that did not work out. Say so plainly, because that saves others the same mistake.

## What does not belong

- Adverts. If you sell the tool, you can still suggest an example, but say that you do, and the write-up must come from the charity or an independent source.
- Anything that names or could identify the people a charity supports. Describe the service, not the individuals.
- Claims with no source. "Saved 50% of staff time" needs a link.

## What happens next

A maintainer reads every proposal and pull request, and aims to reply within two working days. We may edit your wording so entries read alike, and we will ask if anything is unclear. Once it is merged, your example is on the list.

By taking part you agree to the [code of conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md). The list is published under [CC0](LICENSE), which means anyone can reuse it freely, including your entry.

## Questions

Not sure whether something fits? Open a proposal anyway and ask. There are no wrong questions here.
